Pension Catapult · Retirement Tax Minimization Strategy

You’ve won the retirement game. Don’t let the IRS take the last move.

Your IRA or qualified plan is a tax time bomb. Every dollar withdrawn is taxed as ordinary income. Required minimum distributions force withdrawals whether you need them or not. When you pass, your heirs face the same tax burden — often compressed into a 10-year window under the SECURE Act. Remain’s Pension Catapult Strategy — a Retirement Tax Minimization Strategy, or RTMS — repositions those assets into life insurance inside a Grantor Trust, turning a $4M plan into a $13M+ tax-free legacy for the people you love.

Two tracks. One coordinated legacy structure.

Pension Catapult coordinates two asset pools — qualified plan assets and outside liquid assets — into a single Grantor Trust that holds a life insurance policy outside your taxable estate. Here is how the structure assembles.

Track One · Qualified Plan

Reposition the qualified plan assets.

IRA or existing plan assets roll into a defined contribution plan sponsored by your business entity — a non-taxable event that preserves tax-deferred status. The plan then purchases a cash value life insurance policy using pre-tax dollars under DOL PTE 92-6. A $4M plan at a $800K five-pay premium typically acquires a $13M+ death benefit at common issue ages.

  • Rollover to DC plan is a non-taxable event
  • Plan purchases policy with pre-tax dollars
  • 5-pay premium funds a 3–5× death benefit multiplier
Requires $1M+ in a qualified plan and a sponsoring business entity.
Track Two · Outside Assets

Gift or loan to the Grantor Trust.

Liquid assets outside the plan — typically $500K or more — are gifted or loaned to a Grantor Trust that is designed to hold the repositioned policy. The trust becomes the structural vehicle: the policy is transferred out of the plan into trust ownership at fair market value under Rev. Proc. 2005-25, removing it from RMD calculations and from the taxable estate.

  • Grantor Trust receives outside liquid assets
  • Trust takes ownership of the policy at FMV
  • Policy is removed from the taxable estate
Requires $500K+ in outside liquid assets and completed trust documents.
Result · Tax-Free Legacy

Multiplied legacy. Tax-free.

The death benefit passes to heirs free of income tax by operation of IRC §101(a), and free of estate tax because the policy is held outside the grantor’s estate. RMDs on the original plan assets are dramatically reduced. The compressed SECURE Act 10-year window that would have crushed a traditional IRA inheritance no longer applies to the repositioned portion.

  • Death benefit income-tax-free to heirs
  • Estate-tax-free via Grantor Trust ownership
  • RMDs dramatically reduced on repositioned assets
Outcome Typically 3–5× the original plan value, passed on tax-free.

Pension Catapult fits the owner who has already won.

Pension Catapult is for people whose qualified plan assets are excess to their retirement income needs — who want those assets to multiply for the next generation instead of eroding under forced distributions.

  1. i. You’re a business owner between 55 and 72 with a SEP-IRA, solo 401(k), or defined benefit plan that has grown well beyond what you’ll ever spend in retirement.
  2. ii. You’re a corporate executive with a large 401(k) or pension balance, substantial outside investments, and growing concern about the tax burden your heirs will face.
  3. iii. You’ve inherited a large IRA and face the SECURE Act 10-year distribution window — a compressed, forced taxable event you’d like to blunt.
  4. iv. You have at least $1M in qualified plan assets, $500K in outside liquid assets, and a sponsoring business entity — the three structural prerequisites.

Four converging pressures. A narrowing window.

Four forces shape the timing. One is a fixed date on your own calendar; the others are set by Congress and can move.

Pressure 01

RMDs Begin at 73 or 75

Required minimum distributions force you to withdraw — and pay ordinary income tax on — a growing percentage of your plan every year, whether you need the money or not. The window to reposition before RMDs begin is finite and closes on a specific birthday.

Pressure 02

Tax Rates Are Historically Low

Today’s individual income tax rates are low by historical standards, and no rate schedule is guaranteed to last. Repositioning while rates are low settles the tax cost of the transfer under rules you can see, instead of leaving it to whatever applies when your heirs withdraw.

Pressure 03

Estate Exemptions Can Change

The federal estate tax exemption is historically high, but it is set by Congress and has been raised and lowered many times. A policy held in a properly structured trust sits outside the taxable estate regardless of where the exemption lands.

Pressure 04

The SECURE Act 10-Year Window

Under the SECURE Act, most non-spouse beneficiaries must fully distribute an inherited IRA within ten years. Without a strategy, this creates a compressed tax event that forces heirs into higher brackets. Pension Catapult removes the plan assets from that collision course.

Authorized under DOL Prohibited Transaction Exemption 92-6 (as amended) and Rev. Proc. 2005-25, which permit the purchase and transfer of life insurance contracts within qualified plans at fair market value.

Common questions.

What is Pension Catapult?

Pension Catapult is Remain’s Retirement Tax Minimization Strategy — an asset repositioning strategy that moves qualified plan assets into cash value life insurance funded with pre-tax dollars, then transfers the policy outside the plan into a Grantor Trust. The result is dramatically reduced required minimum distributions, lower income taxes, and a death benefit that often runs three to five times the original plan value, passing to heirs free of income and estate tax.

What is the legal authority?

The strategy relies on DOL Prohibited Transaction Exemption 92-6, as amended, and Revenue Procedure 2005-25, which together permit the purchase and transfer of life insurance contracts within qualified plans at the policy’s fair market value. Clean design, a qualified plan administrator, and coordination with your CPA and estate attorney keep the structure well inside the lines.

Who is a good candidate?

The strongest fit is a business owner age 55 to 72 with at least $1M in an IRA or qualified plan that is excess to retirement income needs, at least $500K in outside liquid assets, and a business entity that can sponsor the plan. Corporate executives with large 401(k) balances and inherited IRA holders facing the SECURE Act 10-year window are also strong candidates.

Why is a business entity required?

The strategy routes assets through a qualified defined contribution plan, which requires an employer sponsor. If you own a business entity — LLC, S-Corp, or C-Corp — that entity becomes the plan sponsor. Without an entity, the structural anchor for the strategy does not exist.

Why act before required minimum distributions begin?

Required minimum distributions begin at age 73 (75 if you were born in 1960 or later) and force taxable withdrawals whether you need the income or not. Executing the repositioning before RMDs begin preserves optionality on the plan assets and starts the life insurance funding in the years when you still control the timing of distributions.

What illustrative economics do you typically see?

For a representative 63-year-old with a $4M qualified plan and $1.66M in outside liquid assets, a five-pay premium of roughly $800K inside the plan can acquire a death benefit near $13.3M at common underwriting classes.
